The investigation

Who was to blame?

  • INSAG-1 (T+5 months): the operators.
  • INSAG-7 (T+6 years): the RBMK design - and a deeper failure of “safety culture.”

INSAG - International Nuclear Safety Advisory Group.

The wider cost

A system falls

  • T+5 years (1991) - the Soviet Union dissolves.
  • The botched cover-up eroded public trust and catalyzed glasnost (GLAHZ-nost, “openness”) and its companion reform perestroika (peh-ruh-STROY-kuh, “restructuring”). Openness gained real ground after Chernobyl - and once the system’s secrets opened, the system itself couldn't survive.
  • Gorbachev, 1996: “Chernobyl was perhaps the real cause of the collapse of the Soviet Union, five years later.”

One disaster, many lessons

What does it teach?

  • Systems fail when safety is designed to be assumed, not enforced. Reactor 4’s known flaws - the positive void coefficient, the graphite-tipped rods - were never made safe because they were never meant to fail.
  • Disasters are a chain of small failures, not one cause.
  • Blame gets shifted to protect ego, not to find truth.
  • The cost is paid in people.

Takeaway

What decides whether a system is safe isn’t technical - it’s whether the people who see a problem are allowed to say so.
